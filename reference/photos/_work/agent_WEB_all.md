**Timeline and Process:**
Reconstruction of the Palace of Fine Arts began on July 20, 1964. Workers removed original design elements from which molds were made, and the rotunda, colonnade and all except the steel framework of the gallery were torn down and replaced with concrete castings.

**Construction Methods:**
The colonnades and rotunda were constructed of poured-in-place concrete with precast concrete used for the details and architectural sculpture. The colonnades and rotunda are accurate reproductions of the original structures, with details taken from castings of the original or from original drawings.

**Architects and Designers:**
The 1964–1967 recreation was designed by Hans U. Gerson and Elton Becket and Associates.

**Walter Johnson's Role:**
Community fundraising raised $7.4 million including $2 million from philanthropist Walter Johnson and a $1.8 million city bond. Johnson was a major financial contributor to the reconstruction project.

The first stage of reconstruction was completed by 1967, with the overall project continuing until 1974.

The 2013 restoration project was comprehensive, with work scope including seismic upgrades to the rotunda and colonnades, restoration of the walkways around the facility and the edge of the lagoon, electrical and mechanical upgrades to help improve circulation to the water of the lagoon, and landscaping and other treatments to preserve the history and beauty of the Palace of Fine Arts.
